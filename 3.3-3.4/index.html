Marginals & Conditionals of a Multivariate Gaussian

An interactive companion to Murphy, Probabilistic Machine Learning, §3.2.3 (Eq. 3.26–3.28)
The boxed result
$$ p(y_1) = \mathcal{N}(y_1 \mid \mu_1, \Sigma_{11}) \qquad p(y_2) = \mathcal{N}(y_2 \mid \mu_2, \Sigma_{22}) $$
$$ p(y_1 \mid y_2) = \mathcal{N}(y_1 \mid \mu_{1|2}, \Sigma_{1|2}) $$
$$ \mu_{1|2} = \mu_1 + \Sigma_{12}\Sigma_{22}^{-1}(y_2-\mu_2) \;=\; \mu_1 - \Lambda_{11}^{-1}\Lambda_{12}(y_2-\mu_2) \;=\; \Sigma_{1|2}\big(\Lambda_{11}\mu_1 - \Lambda_{12}(y_2-\mu_2)\big) $$
$$ \Sigma_{1|2} = \Sigma_{11} - \Sigma_{12}\Sigma_{22}^{-1}\Sigma_{21} \;=\; \Lambda_{11}^{-1} $$
Below, y1 is the horizontal axis and y2 is the vertical axis of a 2‑D Gaussian (the smallest case where these equations are non‑trivial). The shaded region is the joint density p(y1, y2). Drag the horizontal line to set an observed value y2 = c and watch the conditional p(y1 | y2 = c) update on the right — exactly per Eq. 3.28.
